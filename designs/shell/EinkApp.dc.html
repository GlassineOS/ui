Pi
From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The number π is a mathematical constant, approximately 3.14159, defined as the ratio of a circle’s circumference to its diameter. It appears in many formulae across mathematics and physics.
π is an irrational number: it cannot be written exactly as a ratio of two integers, and its decimal expansion never ends or settles into a repeating pattern.
History
Ancient civilisations needed fairly accurate values of π for practical work. Around 250 BC, Archimedes bounded it by inscribing and circumscribing polygons around a circle.
